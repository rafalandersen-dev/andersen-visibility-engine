## Strategic thesis

Milo should **not** try to become another Semrush or Ahrefs.

The target category is:

> **Milo — Your AI Growth Operator for small business.**

The product promise is:

> **See where your business is losing visibility in Google and AI. Fix what matters. Prove what changed.**

The product loop is:

> **SEE → DECIDE → DO → PROVE**

### SEE

Observe the business across the few sources that matter most:

- Google Search Console / organic search performance;
- live AI-answer visibility and citations;
- website/content evidence;
- competitors;
- later, local/entity/review and referral signals where justified.

### DECIDE

Milo should not dump dozens of alerts on a small-business owner. It should rank a small set of actions using evidence, relevance, confidence, impact and effort.

The default UX question is:

> **What should I do next, and why?**

### DO

Milo or a connected AI agent prepares the work:

- draft content or page changes;
- FAQ/schema/internal-link improvements;
- opportunity/task creation;
- authority/outreach drafts;
- connector-safe drafts.

Sensitive actions stay controlled. AI may prepare and propose; the user remains in control of publish, delete, billing, personal-data and other high-impact actions.

### PROVE

Every meaningful action should have a measurement window. Milo should show observed change after the action, not claim causality it cannot prove.

Preferred language:

> **Observed after this action: impressions +18%, average position 14.2 → 9.7, AI mentions 2/30 → 6/30.**

Avoid unsupported claims such as “Milo caused +18% traffic.”

## Market position

### Initial ICP

Start narrow:

1. owner-led local/service SMBs;
2. wellness, beauty, clinics and appointment businesses;
3. consultants and professional services;
4. marketing generalists inside small businesses.

Small e-commerce is an expansion segment, not the first product-design centre. Agency workflows come after SMB product-market fit unless strong customer evidence pulls them forward.

### Geographic sequencing

1. Sweden + Denmark — AI-forward messaging is credible and adoption is high.
2. Poland — lead with practical value and action, not jargon such as GEO/AEO.
3. Wider EU / UK after activation and retention are stable.

### Positioning contrast

- **Semrush/Ahrefs:** massive data and research systems.
- **AI visibility trackers:** measurement-first dashboards.
- **AI content tools:** production volume.
- **Milo:** measurement → prioritised action → controlled execution → proof.

Milo wins by reducing work and decision complexity, not by owning the largest keyword, backlink or crawl index.

## Competitive intelligence snapshot — 2026-08-20

### Closest direct competitor: SeoVision

SeoVision combines audit, live AI visibility, content, publishing, backlinks, rank tracking and MCP under an “autopilot” positioning.

Source: https://seovision.io/

Strategic implication: live AI visibility is no longer a differentiator by itself. It is becoming table stakes.

Sources:

- https://writesonic.com/

Strategic implication: Milo must make the recommendation-to-action workflow excellent, not merely add more dashboards.

Semrush is combining classic SEO and AI visibility into a single product stack.

Sources:

- https://www.semrush.com/pricing/ai/
- https://www.semrush.com/pricing/semrush-one/

Ahrefs is moving beyond data dashboards into an agentic direction while expanding Brand Radar and MCP/API access. Brand Radar exposes mentions history, citation history, cited domains and custom-prompt data through API/MCP, which makes AI-visibility data increasingly programmable by external agents.

Sources:

- https://ahrefs.com/pricing/

**Roadmap consequence:** Milo cannot rely on “we have an AI agent” or “we track AI visibility” as durable differentiation. The differentiator must be the **SMB-specific action and proof loop**.

### Competitive response rule

Do **not** add a feature because one competitor shipped it.

A competitor change enters Milo planning only when at least two of these are true:

1. it confirms a real customer problem;
2. it strengthens SEE → DECIDE → DO → PROVE;
3. it is supported by customer evidence or is becoming a clear market table stake.

Otherwise: **IGNORE or MONITOR**.

## North Star and KPI model

### North Star Metric

> **Monthly Verified Growth Actions per Active Project (MVGA)**

An action counts as verified only when:

1. it originated from evidence;
2. it was accepted/executed;
3. it is linked to a page, prompt, query or asset;
4. a measurement window exists after execution.

A positive outcome is **not** required for the metric. A non-improvement is still useful evidence and prevents metric gaming.

### Core product KPIs

| Area | KPI / working target |
| --- | --- |
| Acquisition | Free audit → account: 10–15% after funnel stabilises |
| Activation | Account → baseline + first action: >60% |
| Time to value | URL → first meaningful evidence: <10 min, excluding async scan completion |
| Live AI reliability | Scheduled scan success >98% |
| Data completeness | >95% of expected engine runs represented |
| Execution | >60% of active paid projects complete ≥1 action/month |
| Recommendation quality | >40% of recommended actions accepted |
| Proof coverage | >80% of completed actions have proof window within 90 days |
| GSC adoption | >70% of eligible paid projects connected |
| 90-day paid retention | >80% working target, recalibrate from cohorts |
| Monthly logo churn | <4% after cohorts mature |
| Trust | 0 unintended auto-publish incidents |
| Security | 0 cross-tenant data incidents |
| Unit economics | AI/data/crawl COGS ≤15–20% of subscription revenue |

These are management targets, not industry facts. Recalibrate after real cohorts.

## Pre-public-launch gates

Public launch is a **gate**, not a calendar promise. The working target can move if trust, security, data quality or economics are not ready.

### P0 — must be shipped or explicitly verified before public launch

1. **Public audit safety and release containment**
   - close the currently active staging/release outcome or its successor;
   - anti-SSRF, rate limits, bot protection, cost ceilings, provider fallback and observability verified;
   - no unauthenticated path can create uncontrolled external cost.

2. **Server-authoritative commercial controls**
   - billing/entitlements cannot be self-declared by client state;
   - paid plan lifecycle is verified;
   - authenticated AI usage has enforceable hard limits and global budget protection.

3. **Live AI Visibility v1**
   - minimum three high-quality surfaces rather than many weak ones;
   - store prompt, engine/surface, market/language, timestamp, response evidence, brand mentions, competitors and citations;
   - retain history/trend and expose variability/confidence;
   - clearly label methodology and API/consumer-surface limitations.

4. **Google Search Console direct sync + rank movement**
   - direct OAuth/API is the primary path; CSV can remain fallback;
   - clicks, impressions, CTR and average position by query/page;
   - identify movers, CTR opportunities and near-page-one opportunities.

5. **Action-first Dashboard**
   - the first screen answers “what should I do next?”;
   - action cards show evidence, why it matters, confidence/impact, effort and next step;
   - module navigation becomes secondary.

6. **Claude/MCP safe execution layer**
   - read project/evidence/opportunities/GSC/AI visibility/drafts;
   - create/update drafts, opportunities and tasks;
   - propose changes safely;
   - publish/delete/billing/personal-data actions stay blocked or explicitly confirmation-gated.

7. **Proof Loop v0**
   - at least one action type can travel end-to-end:
     `evidence → action → draft/change → publish marker → measurement`.

8. **Economics and observability**
   - cost per scan/project is measured;
   - provider cost and failures visible;
   - no paid tier has negative gross margin at expected normal/high usage.

9. **Launch quality**
   - no open P0 security issue;
   - support/error paths are understandable;
   - core data methodology and limitations are documented;
   - meaningful beta cohort completes the core loop without founder heroics.

### P1 — important soon after launch, but not launch blockers unless customer evidence changes this

- citation/source-gap intelligence;
- deeper competitor intelligence;
- WordPress draft-first publishing hardening;
- prompt discovery from GSC/site evidence;
- Shopify connector hardening;
- lifecycle digests and monthly proof reports;
- recommendation-quality instrumentation.

### P2 — consciously later

- multi-page crawler depth beyond what core actions require;
- additional AI engines after the first three surfaces are trustworthy;
- GBP/local integrations;
- Wix and broader CMS coverage;
- agency portfolio mode;
- API/export for advanced partners.

## Twelve-month roadmap after public launch

The sequence is outcome-based. If a month’s exit criteria fail, fix the outcome instead of automatically moving to the next feature package.

### Month 1 — Make live visibility trustworthy

**Outcome:** users trust the AI visibility baseline and can see why it changed.

Build/experiment:

- citation/source gaps;
- share-of-voice and competitor movers;
- confidence/variability treatment;
- weekly “what changed” digest.

Exit criteria:

- >95% data completeness;
- no material methodology confusion in support/user interviews;
- users can explain what a visibility change means.

**Do not:** add more engines just for a bigger logo list.

### Month 2 — Close the action-to-proof chain

**Outcome:** a recommended action becomes a measurable completed loop.

Build/experiment:

- Proof Loop v1 with 7/28/90-day views;
- WordPress draft-first flow, diffs and rollback markers;
- action-level evidence history.

Exit criteria:

- one high-volume action type works end-to-end reliably;
- >50% of completed core actions acquire a measurement window.

### Month 3 — Remove manual prompt setup

**Outcome:** most new customers do not need to invent tracking prompts.

Build/experiment:

- prompt discovery from site, Brand Intelligence and GSC;
- conversational-query clustering;
- competitor prompt-gap suggestions;
- Shopify connector hardening if e-commerce demand justifies it.

Exit criteria:

- ≥80% of new customers can accept/edit suggested prompts rather than start from blank.

### Month 4 — Activation and retention system

**Outcome:** Milo becomes useful without founder-led onboarding.

Build/experiment:

- URL-first onboarding;
- automatic business/service/competitor discovery;
- lifecycle messages;
- weekly change alerts;
- monthly proof summary;
- churn reason instrumentation.

Exit criteria:

- activation >60%;
- time to first meaningful value <10 minutes where technically possible;
- support load per activated customer trends down.

### Month 5 — Local-service moat

**Outcome:** Milo becomes materially better for local/service SMBs than generic SEO suites.

Build/experiment:

- local service playbooks;
- entity/location/review recommendations;
- GBP integration discovery/foundation;
- vertical guardrails for wellness/clinic/professional-service claims.

Exit criteria:

- at least three vertical playbooks produce measurable customer value;
- at least two publishable case studies or verified beta stories.

### Month 6 — Authority without building a backlink network

**Outcome:** Milo recommends credible authority-building actions without operating a proprietary exchange.

Build/experiment:

- citation/source opportunity model;
- authority-gap recommendations;
- reviewable outreach drafts;
- source-quality scoring;
- Wix connector only if customer demand is real.

Exit criteria:

- users accept authority recommendations at a useful rate;
- no incentive to chase low-quality link volume.

### Month 7 — Agency expansion gate

**Outcome:** decide from evidence whether agency portfolio mode deserves product investment.

Build only if gate passes:

- portfolio view;
- client permissions;
- cross-project proof reporting;
- white-label reporting hardening.

Gate:

- ≥10 credible agency/design-partner accounts requesting the workflow **or** equivalent revenue evidence.

If the gate does not pass, defer and invest in SMB retention instead.

### Month 8 — Recommendation intelligence + unit economics

**Outcome:** Milo becomes better at choosing the next action while protecting margin.

Build/experiment:

- recommendation acceptance learning;
- impact/effort calibration;
- scan cadence optimisation;
- provider/model routing by value/cost;
- action success/neutral/failure learning.

Exit criteria:

- recommendation acceptance >40% target or improving cohort trend;
- core data/AI COGS within target range.

### Month 9 — Case-study and experiment engine

**Outcome:** product-led proof becomes an acquisition asset.

Build/experiment:

- baseline/action/proof case-study templates;
- customer-consented anonymised benchmarks;
- lightweight experiment framework;
- proof widgets/export only if they support acquisition or retention.

Exit criteria:

- repeatable customer proof stories generated without manual data archaeology.

### Month 10 — Partner and distribution layer

**Outcome:** Milo grows through people already serving SMB websites.

Build/experiment:

- partner onboarding for web studios/consultants;
- safe client hand-off;
- API/export surfaces required by real partners;
- referral attribution.

Exit criteria:

- partner-sourced activated accounts show competitive CAC/retention.

### Month 11 — Internationalisation and selective engine expansion

**Outcome:** expand only where data quality and demand justify it.

Build/experiment:

- language/market methodology hardening;
- add Claude/Copilot/Grok/other surfaces only if demand and methodology are credible;
- localised recommendation models/copy.

Exit criteria:

- new market/engine delivers usable evidence, not vanity coverage.

### Month 12 — Pricing, moat and year-two review

**Outcome:** decide what Milo has actually become and invest around proven retention.

Review:

- retention and expansion by ICP;
- pricing and limits;
- MVGA and proof coverage;
- data/AI unit economics;
- strongest verticals;
- competitor movement;
- which recommendation signals have become proprietary learning.

Output:

- year-two strategy;
- keep/kill/scale decisions;
- refreshed category claim backed by customer evidence.

## Moat plan — 12 to 24 months

Milo’s defensibility should come from accumulated workflow intelligence, not commodity generation.

Build toward:

1. **Evidence-action graph** — which signals led to which actions for which type of business.
2. **Outcome history** — what happened after actions, with honest confidence boundaries.
3. **Vertical playbooks** — local/service SMB patterns that general SEO suites do not optimise around.
4. **Safe execution layer** — reviewable agent actions and connector trust model.
5. **Customer context** — Brand Intelligence, claims, services, locations, historical actions and proof.
6. **Recommendation learning** — acceptance and observed-outcome data improves ranking of actions.
7. **Partner distribution** — web studios/consultants operating Milo for client portfolios after core SMB PMF.

Do not call raw AI outputs a moat.

## Agent planning protocol

When Claude, Codex or another agent picks up Milo:

1. **Recover truth first.** Read the canonical documents and current issue/PR evidence.
2. **Do not assume strategy equals shipped state.** Verify code, live environment and external integrations.
3. **Pick one outcome, not a feature dump.** The active outcome should have an Outcome Owner, evidence, acceptance criteria and explicit exclusions.
4. **Prefer the smallest end-to-end loop.** A complete SEE → DECIDE → DO → PROVE slice is more valuable than five disconnected modules.
5. **Protect trust boundaries.** Publishing, billing, personal data, secrets and destructive actions require their defined gates.
6. **Measure cost and customer value together.** Live AI scanning that destroys gross margin is not a product win.
7. **Do not chase competitors automatically.** Apply the competitive response rule above.
8. **Write back after verified change.** Update `CURRENT_STATE.md`, `DECISIONS.md`, `ROADMAP.md` and evidence as appropriate.

### Recommended planning packet for each strategic outcome

Every new strategic issue should state:

- business outcome and customer problem;
- evidence / why now;
- explicit scope and exclusions;
- architecture/trust assumptions;
- success metric and baseline;
- cost ceiling;
- acceptance criteria;
- adversarial/failure tests where relevant;
- rollout/rollback;
- canonical writeback required after completion.
